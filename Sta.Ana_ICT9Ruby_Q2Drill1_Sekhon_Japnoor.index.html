Learning for Basketball

Welcome to the basketball learning drill. This page provides information about basketball, its rules, and how to play the game effectively.


About Basketball

Basketball, game played between two teams, typically of five players each, on a rectangular court. Each team tries to score by shooting, laying up, or dunking the ball through the opponent’s goal, an elevated horizontal hoop and net called a basket. The only major sport strictly of U.S. origin, basketball was invented by James Naismith in 1891 and within a decade had spread around the world. Over the course of the 20th century, it became one of the leading competitive sports, organized at the international level by FIBA (founded 1932) and further popularized by the growth of professional leagues, most notably the NBA (formed in 1949) and the WNBA (founded in 1997). Meanwhile, basketball also became a beloved recreational sport, and informal games between two or more players can be found year-round on courts in public parks, municipal, school, and church gyms, and in family driveways.

Basketball Rules

The game is played in four quarters of 10 (international) or 12 (NBA) minutes each. If the score is tied at the end of regulation play, an additional five-minute overtime period is played. Each team has a basket at its offensive end of the court and attempts to score by throwing the ball through the opponent’s basket. The team with the most points at the end of the game wins. A field goal scores two points for the shooting team if the player shoots from within the three-point line, and three points if shot from beyond that line. A free throw is worth one point. The ball can be advanced on the court by passing it to a teammate or by bouncing it while walking or running (dribbling). The ball can be stolen from an opposing player, and players can intercept passes. Players are not allowed to run with the ball without dribbling, and they cannot touch the ball with their feet.

How to become better at Basketball:

To become better at basketball, practice regularly, work on your shooting and dribbling skills, improve your physical fitness, and study the game to understand strategies and plays. Playing with others and receiving feedback can also help you improve your skills.
